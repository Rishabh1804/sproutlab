The advice reversed — early intro PREVENTS allergy

Until ~2008–2015, guidance advised delaying allergens to prevent allergy. Reversed: "no evidence that delaying introduction of allergenic foods… prevents allergies once babies are ready, typically at about 6 months" (AAP). Delaying peanut "may promote rather than prevent food allergies" (WHO-aligned). consensus

LEAP trial — the pivotal evidence NEJM 2015, verified

Avoidance (skin-prick −)13.7%
Early peanut (skin-prick −)1.9%
Avoidance (sensitised)35.3%
Early peanut (sensitised)10.6%

640 high-risk infants randomised to regular peanut vs avoidance to age 5. ~81% overall reduction. LEAP-On showed protection persisted after a 12-month avoidance period.

EAT trial — the dose-and-adherence nuance

1,303 general-population infants, 6 allergens from 3 months vs ~6 months. Intention-to-treat: not significant. Per-protocol (achieved the dose): significant reduction in peanut + egg allergy. Only ~42% met the per-protocol target. NEJM 2016

⚑ The lesson is "introduce AND keep offering." Early introduction is easy to attempt and hard to sustain — tolerance depends on repeated exposure, not a one-time taste. The app should reinforce ongoing offers, gently.

Nutrition — the reinforcing co-benefit

Peanut · almond · cashew
  • Plant protein + healthy fats
  • Almond (badam): vitamin E, magnesium, calcium
  • Cashew (kaju): zinc, iron, magnesium
Walnut (akhrot)
  • Plant omega-3 ALA (~2.5 g/oz) — brain development
  • Complements (doesn't replace) other DHA sources
  • High value in a vegetarian diet
⚑ Don't over-claim. The strongest reason to introduce early is allergy prevention, not a unique nutrient. Lead with prevention; nutrition reinforces. "Valuable," not "essential."

Form is the gate — grinding fixes choking, not allergy

Smooth / ground (SAFE)
introduce early
smooth nut butter thinned; finely ground nut powder/flour mixed into food; soaked-peeled-ground almond paste. Start ~¼ tsp.
Whole / chopped (CHOKING)
never before ~5 yr
whole peanuts, whole/chopped tree nuts, chunky nut butter, or a thick glob off the spoon — all choking hazards.
✓ The design crux. Unlike honey (cooking does NOT remove the spore hazard), for nuts grinding/smoothing genuinely makes the food safe on the choking axis. The allergy axis is separate and is NOT removed by form — that's managed by early introduction + watching.
⚑ Upper-bound divergence. AAP says under 4; NHS says under 5. House rule: the conservative 5 (also governs future grapes/popcorn/raw carrot).

First-exposure protocol

Small amount, one allergen at a time
~¼ tsp smooth/ground, stirred into a tolerated food.
At home, morning or midday
Not at dinner — so you can observe through the day.
Watch ~2 hours
Most reactions appear within this window.
If tolerated — keep offering regularly
A few times a week maintains tolerance (EAT/LEAP-On lesson).
⚑ High-risk branch (NIAID vs AAP). US-NIAID: test high-risk infants (severe eczema / egg allergy) before first peanut. AAP/NHS/ASCIA: introduce ~6mo, no routine prescreen. Default = mainstream; the model surfaces a non-suppressible clinician-first note only for the eczema/egg-allergy cohort.

Know mild from severe

Mild — one body system. Itchy hives/welts, redness or rash; swelling around the mouth/eyes; vomiting, sometimes loose stool, sudden fussiness. → stop, watch closely, call your doctor.
Severe — emergency. Trouble breathing/wheeze; swelling of face/lips/tongue/throat; pale, floppy, or very sleepy; collapse. → Call 112 right away and use a prescribed adrenaline auto-injector.

In babies, behaviour change (suddenly limp, can't hold head up) is a recognised severe sign. 94% of severe infant reactions involve hives; 83% involve vomiting. Anaphylaxis can onset within 5–30 minutes — keep watching across the first several exposures, not just day one.

✓ Reassurance. Most introductions go fine. The point of "small amount, at home, watch ~2h" is exactly so that, in the rare reaction, you spot it early and act — not a reason to avoid the food.

An Indian-vegetarian gift

Groundnut/peanut, badam (almond), akhrot (walnut), kaju (cashew) are everyday foods. In a vegetarian household they are a primary source of protein and healthy fats — making early, regular introduction both culturally natural and nutritionally valuable. staple status

Cultural prep is already a safe form

Traditional soaked-peeled-ground almond (badam) paste — soaked overnight, skin removed, ground smooth, stirred into milk/porridge — is essentially a textbook infant-safe form: smooth, no choking risk, easy to dose. A cultural asset the model can affirm rather than override.

IAP & the honesty line

✓ IAP (India) — choking + form confirmed. Complementary-feeding guidance covers allergens + choking; peanut introduced as a small amount of peanut butter in cereal/fruit/yoghurt or dissolved in milk; whole peanuts/tree nuts not given to babies.
⚑ Do NOT attribute early-intro-prevents-allergy to an Indian body. No India-specific government early-introduction directive was located. The prevention claim is well-sourced internationally (AAP/NHS/ASCIA/NIAID) and culturally compatible — but it must not be laundered onto IAP/MoHFW/FSSAI (the honey-brief lesson). IAP's exact early-intro stance needs primary-PDF extraction first.
✗ Separate food — soya / soya chunks. Soy is its own allergen (also a legume), heavily used in this vegetarian household. Its own future record, NOT folded into peanut/tree-nut.

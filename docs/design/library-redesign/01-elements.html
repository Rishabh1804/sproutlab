3 · Emergency Deck — open · the four never-cross floors (both faces stacked)
Choking
Mechanical · not an allergy
  • silent or weak cough; can't breathe, cry, or make a sound
  • turning blue (gums, lips, nailbeds); clutching the throat or going limp

NO adrenaline. Effective cough → encourage it. Silent / can't breathe, under 1: alternate 5 back blows + 5 chest thrusts — never abdominal thrusts. Not clearing → call 112 / 108; unresponsive → start CPR.

Allergic reaction
Anaphylaxis
  • trouble breathing or wheezing
  • swelling of the face, lips, or tongue
  • going floppy, pale, or very sleepy

Use a prescribed adrenaline auto-injector if you have one and call 112 / 108 right away. An antihistamine does not treat anaphylaxis.

Delayed vomiting
FPIES · hours after eating
  • repeated forceful vomiting with paleness or floppiness a few hours later — not just right away

Seek urgent care even without a rash. FPIES is not an allergy you can see — the vomiting and floppiness are the signs.

Suspected poisoning
Acute toxin · e.g. honey under 1
  • after honey before age 1: a weak cry or weak suck, unusual floppiness, constipation

If your baby has had some and any of these appear, see a doctor promptly.

6 · Open reader — pinned floor (encourage order: benefit → form → floor)
Peanut — good to introduce early, in safe form
Why introduce early

Introducing peanut early and keeping it in the diet helps prevent peanut allergy.

  • High-value plant protein and healthy fats; early, sustained peanut markedly lowers the risk of developing a peanut allergy.
Safe forms
  • smooth peanut butter thinned into food
  • peanut flour / powder stirred in
Never
  • whole peanuts or nuts (choking)

Grinding removes the choking risk, not the allergy risk.

If this happens, it's an emergency
  • trouble breathing or wheezing
  • swelling of the face, lips, or tongue
  • going floppy, pale, or very sleepy

A mild rash alone: stop, watch closely, and call your doctor. Any trouble breathing, face or throat swelling, or floppiness: call emergency services (112) right away and use a prescribed adrenaline auto-injector if you have one.

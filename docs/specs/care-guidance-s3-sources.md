### B4. Hydrocele: ages 0–24 m
| Claim | Source | Quote |
|---|---|---|
| A hydrocele is fluid around a testis that swells the scrotum. It is common in newborn boys. | AAP-HYDRO | "A hydrocele describes fluid that collects and causes swelling of the scrotum around the testicle. Many newborn boys have this problem" |
| It is usually not painful. | AAP-HYDRO; RCH-SCROT | "Hydroceles are usually not painful." / RCH lists hydrocele under "Non-painful scrotal swelling" |
| It may get bigger with crying and smaller lying down, and can change size over weeks. | AAP-HYDRO | "The hydrocele may change size throughout the day, getting worse with crying or straining and then get better at night when your child is lying down." |
| Most go away on their own, often by about the first birthday. | AAP-HYDRO | "it usually goes away by around their first birthday without any treatment" |
| It can look like a hernia, so let the doctor check any scrotal swelling. | AAP-HYDRO; NHS-LUMP | "Hydroceles are very similar to inguinal hernias" / "you should always get them checked by a GP" |
| **See the doctor:** still there after the first birthday. | AAP-HYDRO | "If they have not gone away by one year of age, then your child may need surgery." |
| **Emergency:** sudden scrotal or groin pain with swelling. | AAP-HYDRO | "If your child is having lots of sudden pain in the scrotum or groin and scrotal swelling, it is possible that the testicle has twisted on itself (called testicular torsion) or a piece of intestine is stuck in the groin. … call your pediatrician immediately or take them to the nearest emergency room." |

- ⚠ **Disagree (when to refer).** AAP-HYDRO says "by one year". AAP-HERNIA says "Most can be watched until 12 to 18 months of age". RCH-SCROT says "90% resolve spontaneously by 2 years. Consider outpatient surgical referral for repair if present after 2 years of age." The app says "still there after the first birthday, ask the doctor": the earliest threshold, framed as a question for the doctor rather than a surgery date.
- ⚠ **Spec correction.** The spec says a hydrocele "stays the same size". AAP says a (communicating) hydrocele can change size with crying, which is also a hernia sign. The app doesn't use size change to tell them apart. It says the two look alike and the doctor should check.

- ⚠ **Disagree (when to test).** ISPN-2023 and RCH-UTI use fever without a focus for **more than 48 h** ("Consider UTI in any child if fever without focus persists after 48 hours"). AAP-UTI's parent page says talk to the pediatrician if fever with no other symptoms lasts "more than 3 days". AAP 2011 has no fixed duration and works from risk factors (age, temperature, sex, circumcision status). NICE NG224 defers to its fever-in-under-5s guideline. The app says "ask the doctor whether a urine test is needed — especially if it lasts more than 2 days", which follows the Indian guideline (ISPN) and RCH.
- ⚠ **Spec correction (risk detail).** The spec says "higher for uncircumcised boys under 12 months". The quotable sources support "uncircumcised boys have a higher risk" (SG-2005, ISPN-2011) but not the under-12-months qualifier as a quotable sentence. AAP 2011's risk algorithm is a figure (an image in the AAFP summary), and the guideline full text was not reachable (publications.aap.org returned 403). The app drops "under 12 months".
- **The bag-urine warning is clinician-side and kept out of the app:** AAP 2011 says "the diagnosis of UTI cannot be established reliably by a culture of urine collected in a bag", and RCH-UTI says "Bag urine: not recommended for culture". These are for the doctor, not a parent action.

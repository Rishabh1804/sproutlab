Why it matters — early intro PREVENTS egg allergy

Until ~2008–2015, guidance advised delaying allergenic foods (incl. egg) to prevent allergy. That advice is reversed. AAP/HealthyChildren now states there is no evidence that delaying allergenic foods like egg prevents allergies once a baby is ready, typically around 6 months. Delaying does not prevent allergy and may increase it. consensus

PETIT & EAT — RCT reductions PubMed-verified

PETIT placebo38%
PETIT heated egg8%
EAT per-protocol (std)5.5%
EAT per-protocol (egg)1.4%

PETIT (Natsume et al., Lancet 2017; PMID 27939035): high-risk eczematous Japanese infants on stepwise heated whole-egg powder (50 mg/day 6–9mo → 250 mg/day 9–12mo) plus aggressive eczema treatment — egg allergy at 12mo 8% vs 38% placebo, P=0.0001. EAT (Perkin/Lack, NEJM 2016; PMID 26943128): per-protocol 1.4% vs 5.5%, P=0.009; intention-to-treat NOT significant (7.1% vs 5.6%, P=0.32).

⚑ Two integral conditions of the PETIT result: (a) the studied form was heated/cooked egg, not raw; (b) eczema was actively controlled as part of the protocol. PETIT does not license giving raw egg, nor giving egg to an uncontrolled-eczema infant without care. For significant eczema or a strong family allergy history, early egg is still the goal — but involve a paediatrician on timing and eczema control.
⚑ The EAT lesson is dose + adherence. The benefit is real but depends on getting enough egg in, regularly. Frame egg as "introduce and keep offering," not a one-time tick.

The safe form — fully-cooked egg High

Fully-cooked (SAFE)
introduce early
mashed hard-boiled egg, scrambled until firm, or baked into food (omelette set firm, egg in khichdi/porridge cooked through). Start with about ⅓ of a well-cooked egg.
Runny / raw (AVOID)
not safe in India
runny, soft-yolk, half-boiled or lightly-cooked egg. NHS allows runny egg for infants only with the British Lion mark — India has no equivalent scheme, so cook until both white and yolk are solid.
✓ Why fully-cook. Two reasons compound: (1) salmonella — without a control-scheme assurance, raw/undercooked egg carries an infection risk far more serious in an infant; (2) allergenicity — well-cooking (heating) reduces egg's allergenic potency, which is exactly why PETIT used heated egg powder. Egg has no choking-form gate (it is soft) — the gate it passes through is cooked-state, not particle-size.

Reaction watch & first introduction

Egg is among the most common infant food allergens — but, reassuringly, it is commonly outgrown: a majority of egg-allergic children outgrow it by school age, unlike peanut/tree-nut. The tone here is calm and reassuring, not alarmist.

From mild to emergency

Mild — one body system
itchy hives/welts, redness or rash, swelling (esp. around mouth/eyes), vomiting, loose stool / GI upset, sudden fussiness.
What to do for mild
stop the food, monitor closely, contact your doctor; an antihistamine may be advised by a clinician.
Severe / anaphylaxis — red flags
difficulty/noisy breathing, wheeze, persistent cough; tongue/throat swelling or throat tightness; hoarse voice or cry; pale and floppy (sudden limpness), collapse. Two body systems involved → treat as anaphylaxis.
Act immediately
lay the child flat (do not stand them up); use a prescribed adrenaline auto-injector immediately; call emergency services — 112 (all-India) or 108 (ambulance).

Most reactions appear within minutes to ~2 hours; anaphylaxis often within 5–30 minutes. (ASCIA — First Aid for Anaphylaxis.)

First-exposure protocol

Small amount, fully cooked
a small portion of fully-cooked egg (about ⅓ of a well-cooked egg to build toward).
At home, morning or midday
not at dinner — so you can observe through the day.
Watch afterward
most reactions appear within a couple of hours.
If tolerated — keep offering regularly
the EAT lesson: sustained, regular intake is what carries the benefit.
✓ Reassurance. Most introductions go fine. "Small amount, fully cooked, at home, watch" exists so that, in the rare reaction, you spot it early and act — not a reason to avoid egg.

Indian & cultural context

Egg is a common, affordable, culturally accepted complementary food in India. It appears in the IAP (Indian Academy of Pediatrics) complementary-feeding food groups as a protein-rich infant option. staple status

Cultural prep is already the safe form

Indian egg preparations for infants — well-mashed boiled egg, firm scrambled egg (egg bhurji cooked through, not runny), egg stirred into khichdi/porridge and cooked — are already fully-cooked forms, which is exactly the safe default. A cultural asset the model can affirm. The thing to correct is any practice of giving half-boiled / soft-yolk / runny egg to a baby (no Lion-mark safeguard in India).

Hidden-egg sources (for an avoiding household)

Once a parent is avoiding egg, the failure mode is accidental exposure via an aliased or unlabelled source. Watch labels for: albumin/albumen, ovalbumin, ovomucoid, ovomucin, any "ovo-" prefix, ovovitellin/vitellin, livetin, lysozyme, globulin, conalbumin, "egg solids," "dried/powdered egg."

Hidden-egg foods common in an Indian household: egg-glazed / egg-washed bakery buns and pastries (the shiny glaze is frequently an egg wash), cakes/biscuits/breads, mayonnaise & salad dressings, custard puddings, ice cream, some kheer mixes, egg noodles / hakka noodles, batters, instant pancake/cake mixes, marshmallow, meringue. Lecithin (E322) is usually soy/sunflower but can be egg-derived — worth checking.

⚑ Read the IAP citation correctly. IAP listing egg among complementary-feeding food groups is a nutrition statement (good infant protein/iron/choline source) — NOT an "introduce egg early to prevent allergy" directive. Do not launder the international early-introduction-for-prevention claim onto IAP's name (the honey-brief lesson).
✓ Vegetarian note. In a vegetarian or egg-avoiding household, egg is simply not introduced — a dietary choice, not a safety failure. The prevention benefit is one input, not an obligation; the brief should not push egg on a family that doesn't eat it.
